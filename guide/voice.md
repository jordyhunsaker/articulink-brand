# Voice & writing

Our voice is the personality behind our words. It stays consistent across
all communications; our tone adapts to context.

## Voice traits

| Trait | What it means | Do | Don't |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Warm** | We speak like a trusted friend, not a medical textbook | "Great job today! You're making amazing progress." | "Session completed. Next appointment in 7 days." |
| **Clear** | We explain complex things simply, without jargon | "We'll help your child make clearer 's' sounds." | "We'll address the lateral lisp affecting sibilant phonemes." |
| **Encouraging** | We celebrate progress and keep spirits high | "Every small step forward is a win!" | "Results may vary based on condition severity." |
| **Professional** | We're experts, but we don't talk down to anyone | "Our therapists are certified and experienced." | "Trust us, we know what we're doing." |

## Tone by audience

- **Parents** — reassuring, informative, empathetic. Parents need to feel
  confident in their choice.
- **Children** — playful, encouraging, simple. Therapy should feel like fun,
  not work.
- **Professionals** — knowledgeable, direct, respectful. Peer-to-peer with
  clinical partners.

## Writing tips

1. Use active voice. "We'll call you," not "You will be contacted."
2. Keep sentences short — 15–20 words max.
3. Avoid jargon. If a 12-year-old wouldn't understand it, simplify.
4. Lead with benefits. What does this mean for the reader?
5. Be specific. "In 2 business days," not "soon."
6. End with action. Tell people what to do next.
